# Student Progression and Careers

*Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing T Level → Overview*

This page covers who the qualification is for, what's required alongside the classroom-based Technical Qualification, and where it can lead.

## Who this qualification is for

This qualification is for 16-19-year-old learners who want to work within the engineering industry. There are no formal entry requirements set by City & Guilds, but learners are expected to have appropriate attainment at Level 2 before starting.

It's designed for someone not yet employed, or looking to enter the industry after mainstream education. The qualification gives learners breadth of knowledge and understanding across the engineering industry, alongside the occupational and core skills needed to enter it - and is built to support fair access, letting learners manage and improve their own performance as they go.

## Industry placement and other T Level requirements

The classroom-based Technical Qualification is only one part of the full T Level. To achieve the complete T Level, learners must also meet every other requirement set by the Institute for Apprenticeships and Technical Education (IfATE), including:

- An **industry placement** of 315-350 hours (45-50 days) with an employer.
- Any other IfATE requirements, such as licence-to-practise qualifications where relevant.

Centres and providers work with local employers throughout the course too, not just during the placement - employers contribute demonstrations, talks on the industry, and (where possible) the work placements themselves.

## What the qualification covers

Across the course, learners build understanding of the engineering industry and sector, covering topics such as:

- customer and client requirements
- principles of design
- design processes
- communication in design

Alongside the shared common core, every learner studies **one** standalone Occupational Specialism. Nationally there are four choices (Mechanical engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering, Control and instrumentation engineering, Structural engineering) - this study guide covers **Mechanical engineering only**, the pathway taught at UTC Leeds.

## Threshold competence

The qualification is designed to deliver a high level of engineering-industry knowledge plus the occupational skills needed to enter the industry - known as **threshold competence**. A learner who completes it is well placed to build up to full occupational competence, with the right support and training once in a job.

## Where it can lead

The qualification focuses on the knowledge and skills needed for working in engineering, preparing learners to enter the industry either through direct employment or as an apprentice. Completing it also gives learners the option to progress onto higher education courses and training instead.

## Key terms

- **Threshold competence** - having the core engineering knowledge and skills needed to be ready to develop full occupational competence on the job, rather than being fully competent from day one.
- **Industry placement** - the mandatory 315-350 hour (45-50 day) employer placement required alongside the Technical Qualification to complete the full T Level.
- **Occupational Specialism** - the one standalone specialist pathway (of four nationally) a learner chooses to study for threshold competence in a specific engineering discipline.
