# What Are T Levels, and What Is Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing?

*Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing T Level → Overview*

T Levels are two-year, Level 3 technical study programmes, equivalent to three A Levels, that follow GCSEs and sit alongside A Levels and Apprenticeships as one of three main post-16 routes. This page explains what a T Level is in general, and what the **T Level Technical Qualification in Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing (Level 3)** covers - specifically the **Mechanical Engineering pathway**, which is the only pathway this study guide covers (see [Qualification Structure](02-qualification-structure.md) for why).

## T Levels as a qualification type

T Levels are developed in collaboration with employers and businesses so the content meets the needs of industry and prepares learners for work. The three post-16 options for young people are:

- A Levels
- Apprenticeships
- T Levels

Alongside the Technical Qualification (TQ) itself, every T Level requires learners to complete an **industry placement of 315 to 350 hours (45 to 50 days)**. City & Guilds is responsible for developing and delivering the Technical Qualification, but the full T Level (awarded by the Institute for Apprenticeships and Technical Education, IfATE) also depends on completing the industry placement and any other requirements IfATE sets.

## What is the Technical Qualification (TQ)?

The Technical Qualification is the main classroom-based part of the T Level. The Outline Content behind it was produced by T Level panels of employers and professional bodies, based on the same standards used for Apprenticeships, and City & Guilds has since amplified that Outline Content into the full specification.

The specification uses a consistent structure throughout:

- **Criteria** - the subject or topic that needs to be delivered and assessed, often supported by a **Range** that lists exactly what must be covered (for example, under "Design processes" the Range lists which specific processes need to be taught).
- **What do learners need to learn?** - context on the depth and breadth a topic needs to be taught to.
- **Skills** - a mapping reference showing which core, maths, English and digital skills are embedded in that piece of content.

## Who is this qualification for, and what does it cover?

This qualification is for 16-19 year-old learners who want to work in the engineering industry. It is designed to deliver a high level of knowledge about the engineering industry alongside the occupational skills needed to enter it - known as **threshold competence**. A learner who completes it is well placed to develop full occupational competence with further support and training once in employment.

Topics covered include customer and client requirements, principles of design, design processes, and communication in design. After the shared common core, learners choose **one** standalone Occupational Specialism:

- Mechanical engineering
- Electrical and electronic engineering
- Control and instrumentation engineering
- Structural engineering

Centres work with local employers, who contribute demonstrations, talks and (where possible) work placements.

## What could this qualification lead to?

This Technical Qualification focuses on the knowledge and skills needed to work in the engineering industry, preparing learners to enter it through employment or an Apprenticeship. Completing the qualification also gives learners the opportunity to progress onto higher education courses and training. It particularly suits someone not yet employed in the industry, or looking to enter it after mainstream education - giving broad knowledge and understanding across engineering, plus the occupational and core skills needed to enter the industry.

The content is set by a representative panel of employers from across the sector, so it prescribes the minimum knowledge and skills genuinely required to enter the industry, and the specification itself is approved by IfATE.

## Key terms

- **Threshold competence** - having the core knowledge and skills needed to enter the occupation and industry, and being well placed to develop full occupational competence with further support once in employment.
- **Technical Qualification (TQ)** - the classroom-taught, examined/assessed component of a T Level.
- **Outline Content** - the employer-panel-defined content framework (based on Apprenticeship Standards) that City & Guilds turns into the full specification.
- **Occupational Specialism** - the one standalone specialism (of four) a learner chooses to study alongside the common core.
- **Industry placement** - the mandatory 315-350 hour (45-50 day) employer placement that runs alongside the Technical Qualification.
