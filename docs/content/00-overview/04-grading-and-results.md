# Grading and Results

*Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing T Level → Overview*

This page explains how the Technical Qualification's two components are graded, and how they combine into the overall T Level programme grade.

## How the components are graded

| Component | Grading scale |
|---|---|
| Core | A\*-E |
| Occupational Specialism | Pass, Merit, Distinction |

## Core component grading

The core component grade is calculated by aggregating points across all the core's assessment components. City & Guilds publishes grade descriptors for each grade band; broadly:

- An **A** grade shows comprehensive understanding of the full range of engineering principles, applied confidently to both routine and non-routine contexts; clear, well-justified, logically-linked responses to complex briefs; meticulous selection of processes, tools, materials, methods and health & safety considerations; a range of communication strategies adapted to audience; and high accuracy with genuine, critical self-evaluation.
- An **E** grade shows only limited understanding of some key principles in routine contexts; general, sometimes superficial links between knowledge and problems, with partial reasoning; little awareness of how decisions impact design, manufacture or maintenance; a narrow range of communication strategies not always suited to the audience, with technical inaccuracies; and only some evaluation of performance, without guidance on how to improve.

Candidates must complete every component to be awarded the Technical Qualification. Anything City & Guilds determines doesn't meet the standard is recorded as **Unclassified (U)**.

## Occupational Specialism grading

The Occupational Specialism grade is based on grade boundaries set for **Pass** and **Distinction**, using judgemental evidence against the specialism's grade descriptors, review of Guide/Grade Standard Exemplification Materials, and statistical evidence. Pass and Distinction grade descriptors are published in the learner and centre occupational assessment materials.

To achieve the Occupational Specialism, a learner must be recognised at **threshold competence (Pass)** - meaning they:

- are well placed to develop full occupational competence, with further support and development, once in employment;
- are as close to full occupational competence as can reasonably be expected from classroom-based study (classroom, workshops, simulated working, and supervised working environments where appropriate);
- have achieved at least a Pass in the relevant Occupational Specialism component.

If a learner doesn't meet the minimum standard for either the core component or the Occupational Specialism, they receive an **Unclassified (U)** grade for that component.

## Awarding the overall T Level grade

To achieve the full T Level (not just the Technical Qualification), a learner must complete every element of the IfATE T Level framework: the Technical Qualification, the industry placement, and any other set requirements (such as a licence-to-practise qualification).

The overall T Level Technical Qualification grade combines the core component grade and the Occupational Specialism grade:

| Core grade | OS: Distinction | OS: Merit | OS: Pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| A\* | Distinction\* | Distinction | Distinction |
| A | Distinction | Distinction | Merit |
| B | Distinction | Merit | Merit |
| C | Distinction | Merit | Pass |
| D | Merit | Merit | Pass |
| E | Merit | Pass | Pass |

## Key terms

- **Unclassified (U)** - the result recorded when a learner doesn't meet the minimum standard for a component.
- **Grade descriptor** - City & Guilds' published description of what a candidate must demonstrate to achieve a given grade band.
- **Guide/Grade Standard Exemplification Materials** - reference materials used to help set and maintain consistent Occupational Specialism grade boundaries.
