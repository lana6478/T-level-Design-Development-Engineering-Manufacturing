# Occupational Specialism Overview

*Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing T Level → Occupational Specialism → Overview*

This T Level offers four occupational specialism pathways nationally: Mechanical Engineering, Electrical and Electronic Engineering, Control and Instrumentation Engineering, and Structural Engineering. **This study guide covers Mechanical Engineering only**, as that's the pathway taught at UTC Leeds - the other three are not included here.

## What this specialism is about

The Mechanical Engineering specialism is worth 680 Guided Learning Hours and is assessed by a practical assignment. Its purpose is for learners to know and undertake designing and development processes within mechanical engineering - planning, performing and evaluating their own work using a range of techniques, methods and resources.

Learners develop knowledge and skills in:

- design methodologies and processes
- the tools, equipment and materials used in mechanical engineering
- mathematical theories and methods used in mechanical engineering
- producing mechanical drawings and representations
- designing and developing working models
- testing models and prototypes

Learners might approach the specialism by asking themselves questions such as: what different types of mechanical systems are there and what are they used for? What do mechanical engineers do on a daily basis? What areas of the engineering industry do mechanical engineers work in?

## Underpinning knowledge and Performance Outcomes

On completion of the specialism, learners understand:

1. **Mechanical engineering knowledge criteria** - see [Knowledge Criteria](01-knowledge-criteria.md).

And are able to (the five Performance Outcomes, all covered together in [Practical Criteria](02-practical-criteria.md)):

2. **Analyse and interpret** engineering and manufacturing requirements, systems, processes, technical drawings and specifications.
3. **Evaluate** systems, designs, components and processes, managing and integrating design information, proposals and specifications, to develop and improve mechanical engineering and manufacturing proposals and solutions.
4. **Propose and design** mechanical engineering and manufacturing systems, products, components, processes and solutions, considering requirements, constraints and context.
5. **Collaborate** to help manage, develop, test and quality assure mechanical engineering and manufacturing design information, systems, processes and outcomes.
6. **Communicate** proposals, design information and solutions, producing, recording and explaining engineering and manufacturing representations, systems, processes, outcomes, specifications and technical drawings.

Completing the specialism also gives learners the chance to develop their maths, English and digital skills - see [Competency Frameworks](../03-appendices/01-competency-frameworks.md).

## How it's assessed

The specialism is assessed by **one practical assignment**, lasting 33 hours 40 minutes, made up of four tasks (design, manufacture and test, peer review, evaluation and implementation). See [Scheme of Assessment](03-scheme-of-assessment.md) for the full breakdown.

## Key terms

- **Performance Outcome (PO)** - one of the 5 numbered practical/skills outcomes (PO2-PO6) learners are assessed against.
- **Outcome 1** - the underpinning knowledge outcome ("Mechanical engineering knowledge criteria"), assessed through how it's applied across the practical assignment rather than as a separate written test.
- **Range** - the source specification's term for the specific scope (materials, methods, standards, etc.) a subtopic covers.
