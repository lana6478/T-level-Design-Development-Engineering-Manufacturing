# Core Component Overview

*Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing T Level → Core Component*

The **engineering common core** (component 300) is the shared foundation every student studies before choosing an occupational specialism. It's worth 680 Guided Learning Hours and is designed as an introduction to engineering and manufacturing that helps learners choose their specialist pathway, covering the theoretical knowledge and practices common across the whole sector. It's assessed through two externally-set exams plus an Employer-set project.

## Purpose

The common core builds the knowledge base every engineering role draws on, whatever specialism a student later chooses: how the sector works, essential maths and science, materials, mechanical/electrical/mechatronic principles, control systems, quality, health and safety, business awareness, and the professional skills to plan and deliver a project. Learners are encouraged to approach it through questions like "what are the different sectors in engineering and manufacturing?", "what mathematics and science are involved?", and "how do I manage and present a project?".

## The 17 underpinning knowledge outcomes

On completion of the common core, learners understand:

1. Working within the engineering and manufacturing sectors
2. Engineering and manufacturing past, present and future
3. Engineering representations
4. Essential mathematics for engineering and manufacturing
5. Essential science for engineering and manufacturing
6. Materials and their properties
7. Mechanical principles
8. Electrical and electronic principles
9. Mechatronics
10. Engineering and manufacturing control systems
11. Quality management
12. Health and safety principles and coverage
13. Business, commercial and financial awareness
14. Professional responsibilities, attitudes and behaviours
15. Stock and asset management
16. Continuous improvement
17. Project and programme management

Topics 1-8 are covered on [Engineering Core, Part 1](01-engineering-core-part1.md); topics 9-17 on [Engineering Core, Part 2](02-engineering-core-part2.md). Completing the common core also gives learners the opportunity to develop their maths, English and digital skills - see [Competency Frameworks](../03-appendices/01-competency-frameworks.md).

## How it's assessed

| Part | What it covers | Weighting |
|---|---|---|
| [Exam Paper 1](04-core-scheme-of-assessment.md) | Maths, science, materials, mechanical/electrical principles, mechatronics (topics 4-9) | 35% of core |
| [Exam Paper 2](04-core-scheme-of-assessment.md) | Sector context, representations, control systems, quality, health & safety, business, professional behaviours, stock/asset management, continuous improvement, project management (topics 1-3, 10-17) | 35% of core |
| [Employer-set Project](03-employer-set-project.md) | A synoptic, industry-style project drawing on the whole core | 30% of core |

The common core is graded overall A*-E (or Ungraded). After the core comes the [Occupational Specialism](../02-occupational-specialism/00-overview.md) - for this guide, that's Mechanical Engineering.

## Key terms

- **Common core** - the shared knowledge component (300) every student studies regardless of specialism, worth 680 GLH.
- **GLH** - Guided Learning Hours.
- **Underpinning knowledge outcome** - one of the 17 numbered topic areas the common core is organised into.
- **Threshold competence** - the level of knowledge and skill needed to be ready to develop full occupational competence on the job.
