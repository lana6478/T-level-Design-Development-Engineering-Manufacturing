# Core Scheme of Assessment

*Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing T Level → Core Component → Core Scheme of Assessment*

The core component is assessed through two externally set, externally marked exams (worth 35% of the core each) and the [Employer-set Project](03-employer-set-project.md) (worth 30% of the core). The whole core component is graded on the A*-E scale, plus Ungraded (U).

## Core exam structure

| Paper | Method | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Marking |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exam paper 1 | Externally set exam | 2.5 hours | 100 | 35% | Externally marked |
| Exam paper 2 | Externally set exam | 2.5 hours | 100 | 35% | Externally marked |
| Employer-set project | Externally set project | 18.5 hours | 90 | 30% | Externally marked |

The exams are made up of short answer questions (SAQs, some structured) and extended response questions (ERQs), sat under invigilated examination conditions. Question difficulty increases through each paper, from lower-demand questions at the start to higher-demand questions at the end. Learners who need to retake the core exams must retake both exams in the same assessment window, within one year of completing their T Level programme.

### What each paper covers

**Paper 1** draws on: [essential mathematics](01-engineering-core-part1.md#4-essential-mathematics-for-engineering-and-manufacturing), [essential science](01-engineering-core-part1.md#5-essential-science-for-engineering-and-manufacturing), [materials and their properties](01-engineering-core-part1.md#6-materials-and-their-properties), [mechanical principles](01-engineering-core-part1.md#7-mechanical-principles), [electrical and electronic principles](01-engineering-core-part1.md#8-electrical-and-electronic-principles), and [mechatronics](02-engineering-core-part2.md#9-mechatronics).

**Paper 2** draws on: [working within the sector](01-engineering-core-part1.md#1-working-within-the-engineering-and-manufacturing-sectors), [engineering and manufacturing past/present/future](01-engineering-core-part1.md#2-engineering-and-manufacturing-past-present-and-future), [engineering representations](01-engineering-core-part1.md#3-engineering-representations), [control systems](02-engineering-core-part2.md#10-engineering-and-manufacturing-control-systems), [quality management](02-engineering-core-part2.md#11-quality-management), [health and safety](02-engineering-core-part2.md#12-health-and-safety-principles-and-coverage), [business/commercial/financial awareness](02-engineering-core-part2.md#13-business-commercial-and-financial-awareness), [professional responsibilities/attitudes/behaviours](02-engineering-core-part2.md#14-professional-responsibilities-attitudes-and-behaviours), [stock and asset management](02-engineering-core-part2.md#15-stock-and-asset-management), [continuous improvement](02-engineering-core-part2.md#16-continuous-improvement), and [project and programme management](02-engineering-core-part2.md#17-project-and-programme-management).

## Assessment objectives (core exams)

| AO | Description | Weighting |
|---|---|---|
| AO1a Demonstrate knowledge | Basic recall of relevant knowledge in response to straightforward questions, e.g. material properties. | 10% |
| AO1b Demonstrate understanding | Explaining principles and concepts beyond recall of definitions, in a general way outside a particular context, e.g. simple engineering terms and concepts. | 22% |
| AO2 Apply knowledge and understanding to different situations and context | Applying understanding of generalities to specific situations, e.g. applying a single concept or an essential mathematical concept to a straightforward situation. | 46% |
| AO3 Analyse and evaluate information and issues | Analysing the interrelated issues in a complex scenario and evaluating them to propose a best solution or predict impacts, e.g. evaluating material properties and requirements for an engineered product. | 22% |

All three AOs require the ability to recall knowledge, but they build on each other: AO1 covers basic recall plus explaining/summarising concepts in the learner's own words; AO2 is about applying that understanding to specific, concrete (if fairly granular) situations; AO3 is the most demanding, requiring analysis and evaluation of a detailed or complex scenario, problem or data set, with marks awarded for the quality and range of the analysis.

For the Employer-set Project's own assessment objectives (AO1-AO5, different from the core exam AOs above), see [Employer-set Project](03-employer-set-project.md).

## Key terms

- **SAQ / ERQ** - short answer question / extended response question, the two main question formats in the core exams.
- **AO** - Assessment Objective, a weighted criterion markers use to grade a piece of assessed work.
- **Ungraded (U)** - the below-a-pass outcome on the core component's A*-E grading scale.
