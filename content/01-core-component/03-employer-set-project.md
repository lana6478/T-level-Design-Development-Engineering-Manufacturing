# Employer-set Project

*Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing T Level → Core Component → Employer-set Project*

The Employer-set project (ESP) is one of three assessment components in the core, worth 30% of the core and 18.5 hours of assessment time. It's externally set and externally marked by City & Guilds, and it's designed to make learners identify and use, in an integrated way, an appropriate selection of skills, techniques, concepts, theories and knowledge drawn from across the whole engineering common core content.

## What the project involves

The ESP consists of a well-defined, real industry-style brief. The brief is complex and non-routine, and requires learners to use relevant maths, English and digital skills to respond to it. It gives learners a valid context to demonstrate their knowledge of the core content and their core skills by solving occupationally relevant situations or problems. Each project is developed together with employers to reflect realistic types of developments, activities and challenges in the sector.

The project is made up of several tasks, all relating to the same ESP brief, covering:

- **Research**
- **Design**
- **Plan**
- **Present**

It draws on knowledge from the core content shared across all four Design and Development specialisms (specialism-specific knowledge and skills are instead assessed separately in each specialism's practical assignment - for this guide, see [Mechanical Engineering: Practical Criteria](../02-occupational-specialism/02-practical-criteria.md)). The project is also linked to four core skills: planning and preparation, communication, develop and manufacture, and evaluation.

## How it's delivered

Projects are released to centre staff in advance of each assessment window. City & Guilds provides centres with assessment windows in which to timetable assessment sessions, following the timings set out in the ESP centre guidance. Centres must keep all live assessment materials secure until the assessment window opens - projects are password-protected and released through a secure method. Guidance on equipment, resources and duration is released in advance so centres can plan delivery.

Learners who fail the ESP on first submission can retake it in any assessment window, provided the retake is completed within one year of finishing the T Level programme.

## Assessment objectives

| AO | Focus | Weighting |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Plan approach to meeting the brief - evidence of a planned, sequenced, prioritised and iterative approach; a clearly structured, cohesive response; logical referencing/research/sourcing; meeting the brief's parameters; effective use of presentation time. | 13% |
| AO2 | Apply core knowledge and skills as appropriate - linking knowledge, principles and ideas and applying them in the context of the brief (materials, concepts, etc.), and applying core skills like communication and planning throughout the project's tasks. | 50% |
| AO3 | Select relevant techniques and resources to meet the brief - choosing effective, appropriate techniques and resources, and using them accurately and with understanding. | 13% |
| AO4 | Use maths, English and digital skills - correct terminology/abbreviations/units, considering the audience (technical vs. non-technical wording), appropriate use of calculations/diagrams, and appropriate use of ICT/digital methods in both the response and its presentation. | 10% |
| AO5 | Realise project outcome and review how well the outcome meets the brief - analysing and evaluating the outcome, drawing conclusions, identifying solutions to the brief's problem, and evaluating other options considered and why they were rejected. | 13% |

*Weightings are rounded to the nearest whole number.*

## Key terms

- **ESP** - Employer-set project, the core component's practical, industry-brief-based assessment.
- **AO** - Assessment Objective, one of the weighted criteria markers use to grade a piece of assessed work.
- **Core skills** - the four cross-cutting skills (planning and preparation, communication, develop and manufacture, evaluation) the ESP is designed to assess synoptically.
