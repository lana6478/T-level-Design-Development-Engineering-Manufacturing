# Competency Frameworks

*Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing T Level → Appendices*

Alongside the technical content, this qualification maps a set of **core skills**, plus general **English**, **maths** and **digital** competencies, onto the core and specialism content. These aren't taught as separate lessons - they're signposted against the relevant criteria throughout the specification, wherever there's a natural opportunity to develop them, and each specialism page in this guide notes where a skill applies.

## The four core skills

These four core skills are fundamental to the knowledge, skills and behaviours learners need as they progress beyond the T Level. They've been mapped onto the qualification content in consultation with industry and providers - not every piece of content develops a core skill, but where one does, it's referenced.

- **Core Skill A - Planning and preparation (DD-CSA).** Interpreting and confirming project requirements; planning and scoping project parameters (timescales, resources, costs); developing project plans. In practice: preparing a programme and critical path (e.g. in Microsoft Project), a stock inventory plan, a project organigram defining stakeholder roles, sharing documentation via SharePoint, running a commercial risk review, preparing initial costings/budgets, and working collaboratively and ethically with clients, teams and end users.
- **Core Skill B - Communication (DD-CSB).** Interpreting, using and producing engineering representations and drawings to graphical-language and industry conventions; interpreting and using technical information and media; communicating with technical and non-technical audiences using technology. In practice: sketch designs and 3D models (e.g. SketchUp), AutoCAD drawings for a CNC router, fully-dimensioned isometric drawings (e.g. Solidworks), tender documentation, budget spreadsheets, presentations of material options, and reporting dangerous situations during manufacture.
- **Core Skill C - Developing proposals and concepts (DD-CSC).** Designing proposals to meet set requirements; developing, modelling and revising concepts. In practice: complying with a client's brief, producing risk assessments/method statements/safe systems of work, applying lean manufacturing to minimise waste, developing and re-evaluating prototypes, planning for production, assessing ergonomics/materials/modularity/sustainability, applying manufacturing standards safely, and considering environmental obligations.
- **Core Skill D - Evaluation (DD-CSD).** Carrying out tests, evaluation and analysis; evaluating how well project requirements have been met. In practice: benchmarking and KPIs, applying manufacturing test methods, using prototypes to evaluate a product against its brief, end-of-line inspection, the product realisation process, Six Sigma continuous improvement, and testing against current UK/international standards.

## Key terms

- **Core skill** - one of four cross-cutting skills (Planning and preparation, Communication, Developing proposals and concepts, Evaluation) mapped across the qualification's content.
- **Signposting** - the practice of flagging which specific content areas offer a natural opportunity to develop a given English, maths, digital or core skill.
