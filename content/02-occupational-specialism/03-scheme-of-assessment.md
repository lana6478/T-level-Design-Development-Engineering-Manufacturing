# Scheme of Assessment

*Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing T Level → Occupational Specialism → Scheme of Assessment*

This page covers how the Mechanical Engineering specialism is assessed: the practical assignment's structure, its four tasks, and how each Performance Outcome is weighted.

## Structure

The specialism is assessed by **one practical assignment**, lasting **33 hours 40 minutes**. Learners are assessed against four assessment themes running through the tasks:

- Health and safety
- Design and planning
- Manufacturing
- Reports

## The four tasks

**Task 1 - Design.** Learners plan and design a prototype/model, producing documents and drawings to industry standards showing how they'll develop it in line with the brief, using CAD software to produce engineering drawings and a virtual model. Learners also give feedback on a peer's engineering drawings.

**Task 2 - Manufacture and test.** Learners manufacture and test the prototype/model in line with their planning and designs, working in a clear, logical sequence, safely, and interpreting/recording test results accurately, selecting and using tools/materials/equipment correctly.

**Task 3 - Peer review.** Learners discuss designs with peers to gain feedback that informs adaptations to their own design before implementation, and give constructive critique with recommendations on others' designs.

**Task 4 - Evaluation and implementation.** Learners evaluate their own design and manufacture, recommending adaptations and improvements, justifying their test methods, providing enough information for a third party to implement the model, and justifying how peer feedback was considered and implemented.

## Performance Outcome weighting

Each Performance Outcome is drawn on by specific tasks and evidenced through specific outputs:

| Performance Outcome | Weighting | Tasks it draws on | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| PO2 - Analyse and interpret requirements/systems/processes/drawings/specifications | 16% | T1, T2, T4 | Design specification, annotated sketches, virtual modelling, design calculations; manufacture of the prototype; evaluation and implementation report |
| PO3 - Evaluate systems/designs/components/processes to develop and improve solutions | 22% | T1, T2, T4 | Engineering drawings, virtual modelling, bill of materials; manufacture of the prototype; evaluation report, revision control document |
| PO4 - Propose and design systems/products/components/processes/solutions | 23% | T1, T2 | Design specification, sketches, engineering drawings, virtual modelling; manufacture and testing of the prototype, records of testing |
| PO5 - Collaborate to manage/develop/test/quality assure | 26% | T2, T4 | Manufacture and testing of the prototype, risk assessment; evaluation report, records of testing, revision control document |
| PO6 - Communicate proposals/design information/solutions | 13% | T1, T4 | Engineering drawings, design specification, annotated sketches, records of testing, virtual modelling; evaluation and implementation report |

Every task also maps onto one or more of the four assessment themes (health and safety, design and planning, manufacturing, reports) - for example, Task 1 evidence is assessed under "design and planning" and "health and safety", while Task 2 evidence sits under "manufacturing" (and often "health and safety" too).

## Delivery resources

Providers are expected to give learners access to appropriate tools, equipment and test instrumentation that reflects current UK industry practice - not just what's convenient to teach with. Suggested textbooks include *Marks' Standard Handbook for Mechanical Engineers*, *Machinery's Handbook*, and *Engineering Project Management*, alongside the Institution of Mechanical Engineers and Health and Safety Executive websites for further reference. Visits to and engagement with local employers/manufacturers are encouraged throughout delivery, not just as one-off events.

## Key terms

- **Assessment theme** - one of the four cross-cutting areas (health and safety, design and planning, manufacturing, reports) that tasks are assessed against.
- **Revision control document** - a record tracking changes made to a design over the course of a project.
