# Practical Criteria

*Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing T Level → Occupational Specialism → Practical Criteria*

This page covers the **Practical criteria for Performance Outcomes 2-6** - the hands-on skills side of the Mechanical Engineering specialism, sitting alongside the underpinning [Knowledge Criteria](01-knowledge-criteria.md). Subsection numbers (2.1, 3.1...) mirror the specification.

## Outcome 2: Analyse and interpret engineering and manufacturing requirements, systems, processes, technical drawings and specifications

**2.1 Access, interpret and respond to design projects and tasks.** Learners access, examine, interpret, review and respond to information from sources such as design briefs, specifications, concept drawings and stakeholders (supervisors, colleagues, clients/end users).

**2.2 Evaluate project expectations and requirements, including risk.** Learners determine and evaluate materials requirements, manufacturing timescale and cost for a specified project, and identify risks (design and/or commercial) to a project along with mitigation actions.

**2.3 Interpret, evaluate and modify engineering information.** Covers a range of formats: specifications, CAD models, engineering drawings, mechanical process and instrument diagrams, standard operating procedures, production plans. Learners interpret information from these sources, identify inaccuracies/errors and how to correct them, and identify and manage scope creep or process-driven design change, escalating or reporting as necessary.

**2.4 Verify mechanical design information against context, function and requirements.** Covers information sources (concept drawings, briefs, specifications) and requirements (components, materials, application, location, risk, environment). Learners interpret design information and evaluate whether a proposal actually meets the requirements.

**2.5 Use technology to evaluate mechanical design elements.** Covers technology (mathematical calculations, CAD simulations, physical modelling) applied to design elements (systems, components, assemblies, products, processes). Learners use appropriate technology, including CAD, to review, analyse and interpret design elements in proposals and products.

## Outcome 3: Evaluate systems, designs, components and processes, managing and integrating design information, proposals and specifications, to develop and improve mechanical engineering and manufacturing proposals and solutions

**3.1 Select and use technology to model and evaluate design performance.** Covers mathematical modelling tools, spreadsheets, and CAD simulations, evaluated against features, issues and current/potential performance. Learners weigh cost, availability, compatibility, training needs and time when selecting a tool, and choose the tool most relevant to the failure modes being assessed.

**3.2 Analyse factors affecting mechanical designs and justify recommendations.** Covers materials, application, location, risk, environment, configuration, geometry and scale. Learners assess and prioritise these factors, compare which apply generally versus only in certain situations, and understand the difference between optimising an existing design and developing a new one.

**3.3 Select and justify materials for specific uses.** Covers the same material families as the knowledge criteria (ferrous/non-ferrous metals, thermoplastics, thermosets, elastomers, composites, engineering ceramics, smart materials). Learners use appropriate information sources on material properties and justify a material choice with reference to context.

**3.4 Apply knowledge of material degradation and failure to evaluate proposals.** Covers chemical attack/corrosion, fracture, mechanical abrasion, creep and fatigue. Learners apply this knowledge, plus prevention/mitigation methods, to investigate and evaluate design proposals, processes and outcomes.

**3.5 Review and confirm the relevance, implications and value of test results.** Covers the same non-destructive and destructive testing methods as the knowledge criteria. Learners determine a test's relevance and value, use results to influence the design, and consider the purpose, context, traceability, recording and reporting of tests, modelling and research.

**3.6 Evaluate designs and design processes against application requirements.** Covers quality and performance evaluation against purpose/functionality, environmental/working conditions, and application requirements like cost and value engineering. Learners assess the fitness for purpose of both the design and the process used to reach it.

**3.7 Compare alternative design options and identify improvements.** Learners compare and evaluate design options, iterations and processes to determine the best solution, using criteria such as fitness for purpose, context, constraints, agreed metrics and specification compliance.

**3.8 Review individual, team and organisational performance across the design and manufacture process.** Covers design methods, quality systems, standards, manufacturing methods, performance targets and KPIs. Learners reflect on and identify potential improvements.

**3.9 Respond constructively and creatively to project/task changes and feedback.** Learners follow a process for confirming the scope of new requirements and the route to revising or resolving them, understand the importance of responding constructively, and log/record changes throughout the design process.

## Outcome 4: Propose and design mechanical engineering and manufacturing systems, products, components, processes and solutions, considering requirements, constraints and context

**4.1 Propose and develop designs to address requirements.** Covers requirements from the design brief, specification, mechanical principles (scientific, mathematical) and material properties. Learners take ideas and sketch them into detailed concepts, annotating to show where requirements are addressed and justifying material choices.

**4.2 Practically model and calculate mechanical system performance.** Covers linear systems (levers, linkages), rotating systems (gears, flywheels) and lifting machines (cranes, pulleys). Learners model and calculate dynamic effects from acceleration, determine rotating-system performance, and analyse lifting-machine characteristics and operation.

**4.3 Produce effective representations using CAD software, tools and technology.** Covers sketching (freehand and isometric), 2D and 3D drawings, models, simulations and accurate 3D assemblies, using 3D CAD solid modelling and manual techniques. Learners use tools safely and effectively, access support when needed, and judge which representation type suits a given situation (e.g. model versus 2D drawing).

**4.4 Produce detailed engineering drawings to relevant standards and codes.** Covers British Standards, ISO standards, CE marking and engineering bodies (Engineering Council, IET, IMechE, SOE). Learners follow a process to produce accurate, detailed drawings, considering accuracy, tolerances, units, scale and correct symbols.

**4.5 Develop high-quality working models meeting functional and non-functional requirements.** Covers block models, rapid-prototyped/3D-printed models and one-off prototype manufacture, against requirements like dimensions (fit, form), aesthetics, mechanical properties and cost. Learners apply design, manufacturing and production principles, following safe working practices.

**4.6 Use basic hand and powered tools safely to carry out basic engineering processes.** Covers common hand/power tools (screwdriver, hacksaw, hammer, files, Allen keys, spanner, socket set, wrench, glue gun, drill, pliers, rule, set square, protractor, Vernier callipers, micrometres) and processes (wasting, shaping, forming, welding, 3D printing). Learners select and use appropriate tools, carry out pre/during/post-use safety checks (calibration, storage, reporting damage), and must use at least one welding type and one 3D printing type when prototyping.

**4.7 Assemble and disassemble mechanical systems.** Covers assembly (configuration, sequencing, aligning, bending, fixing, mechanical jointing, pre-tensioning, sealing, sequential tightening, threaded jointing/locking devices, functional testing) and disassembly (releasing tension/pressure, proof marking, safe tool use). Learners work safely and follow established protocols, including ensuring appropriate training has been given.

**4.8 Produce high-quality designs of realisable mechanical systems that satisfy requirements.** Covers standards, functional requirements (dimensions, mechanical properties, aesthetics, maintenance) and non-functional requirements (cost, quality). Learners test and evaluate finished products, demonstrate commercial realisability, and check designs can actually be manufactured, operated and maintained.

## Outcome 5: Collaborate to help manage, develop, test and quality assure mechanical engineering and manufacturing design information, systems, processes and outcomes

**5.1 Follow professional standards, management systems, policies and safe working practice.** Covers professional standards (Engineering Council, IMechE, IET) and policies/procedures (workplace policies, SOPs, national/industry health and safety requirements). Learners identify where standards/policies sit, understand the consequences of not following them, and assess/manage/mitigate risk in mechanical engineering contexts.

**5.2 Work effectively with others to agree and complete tasks.** Covers tasks such as design, development, investigation, research, problem solving, testing, validation, quality assurance/control, and producing/interpreting documentation. Learners contribute effectively, respond to others' input, and understand how timeframes, deadlines and dynamic conditions affect a task.

**5.3 Complete detailed risk management analyses in response to project requirements.** Learners carry out risk management and identify mitigation actions in contexts like machining, mechanical handling and high-power applications, respond appropriately to unfamiliar situations, and understand commercial risk ranking models.

**5.4 Develop and test models and prototypes of design proposals.** Covers virtual and physical models (block, rapid-prototyped/3D-printed, one-off prototype manufacture). Learners produce models using different methodologies, test/assess/report/respond to model performance, and carry out concept testing, functional tests, measurements and visual inspection of production prototypes.

**5.5 Produce and quality assure design information to professional standards.** Covers design information (proposals, specifications, engineering drawings, technical information) against professional standards (Engineering Council, IMechE, IET). Learners produce, manage, quality assure and validate this information, and check completed drawings for quality, technical compliance and completeness.

## Outcome 6: Communicate proposals, design information and solutions, producing, recording and explaining engineering and manufacturing representations, systems, processes, outcomes, specifications and technical drawings

**6.1 Produce and manage technical documentation using digital tools and conventions.** Covers technical documentation (design specifications, engineering drawings) and digital tools (CAD software, document management systems, SharePoint, spreadsheets). Learners produce, amend, check, manage and version-control documentation using appropriate conventions and protocols, use collaborative digital tools to manage and quality-assure information, and annotate drawings (geometrical tolerances, limits and fits, surface finishes).

**6.2 Communicate design information and options with technical and non-technical audiences.** Covers design information (specifications, proposals, working drawings, representations, technical/non-technical data, systems, processes, risks, outcomes), audiences (stakeholders, colleagues, clients) and formats (sketches, schemes, detailed drawings, diagrams, models, reports). Learners select formats suited to cost, time, availability, end user and project scale, clearly evidence stages of the design process, and communicate/coordinate design information across audiences.

## Guidance for delivery

Providers are encouraged to arrange visits to and engagement with local industry, employers and manufacturers throughout delivery - local employers presenting recent projects (including problems faced and how they were solved) is suggested, as is learners presenting their own work-placement experiences to peers. Formative assessment can include oral Q&A, peer presentations, and observation of measuring activities; learning can be reinforced through revisiting content, group discussion and peer support within the cohort. Providers must keep delivery aligned with current industry practice - providing appropriate tools, equipment and test instrumentation, and teaching on equipment representative of what's actually used in UK industry.

Suggested resources include *Marks' Standard Handbook for Mechanical Engineers* (Avallone, Baumeister & Sadeh), *Machinery's Handbook* (McCauley), and *Engineering Project Management* (Smith), alongside the Institution of Mechanical Engineers (imeche.org) and Health and Safety Executive (hse.gov.uk) websites.

## Key terms

- **Performance Outcome (PO)** - one of the five numbered practical skill areas (PO2-PO6) this page covers.
- **CAD/CAM** - Computer-Aided Design / Computer-Aided Manufacture; CAD models are often transferred to CAM machines to produce parts.
- **Scope creep** - uncontrolled change or growth in a project's requirements after it has started.
